Cars, Smog & Health

1. How cars make smog

Exhaust gases (NOx and VOCs) react in sunlight to form ground-level ozone. Exhaust, brakes and tires also release fine particles (PM2.5).

2. The health toll

Ozone inflames the airways and triggers asthma attacks. PM2.5 enters the bloodstream and raises the risk of heart attack and stroke. Diesel exhaust is a known human carcinogen (IARC Group 1).

3. Who breathes the most car pollution

Vehicle pollution exposure varies more by race than by income. Researchers link this to decades of highway routing, redlining and housing decisions [8].

Other high-risk groups

45 million+ Americans live within 300 ft of a major road or other transportation facility [9]. Children (46% live where at least one air grade fails [5]), older adults, people with asthma or heart disease, and low-income households face the highest risk. In California, people without a car breathe 19% more vehicle PM2.5 than the state average [6].

The biggest one: national vehicle standards

The 1990 Clean Air Act Amendments prevented an estimated 230,000 premature deaths, 2.4 million asthma attacks and 120,000 ER visits in 2020 alone. Benefits were about $2 trillion against $65 billion in costs, roughly 30 to 1 [19]. Combined US emissions of the six main pollutants fell 78% from 1970 to 2023 [20].

What backfired: Mexico City's license-plate driving bans. People bought second, older and dirtier cars, about 325,000 extra vehicles, and air quality did not improve [18].

What the AI computed: no new modeling. It searched the web, pulled published figures from government agencies, NGOs and peer-reviewed studies, and organized them here. Limits: the figures come from different years (2005–2026), places and methods, so compare them carefully. Figures marked † are cited from the published papers but were not re-checked against the original text during this session.
